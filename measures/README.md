# Measure spec

`measure_spec.json` drives `identify()` (`R/identify.R`), which runs on each sheet after
`harmonise()`. Its goal is to make every record say **what it counts**. Two tables in one
release often share every label and differ only in their title: "Defendants finalised"
vs "Defendants with a guilty outcome", "Sentenced" vs "Unsentenced prisoners". Without
this stage their values can't be told apart. Edit the JSON directly; `tar_make()` reruns
`data_identified` and everything after it when it changes.

Fields set here (`population`, `scope`, `measure`, `rate_standardisation`, …) are added
only where the record doesn't already have them, except for `replace_labels`. Each
added field is listed in the record's `.derived`.

## Sections

**`subject_pattern`.** A regex whose first group is the title's *subject*: the text after
"Table N" (and "FDV", "ANZSOC 2023", "Experimental data –" prefixes, and AIHW numbers
such as "Table S5.12:" or "T2.2:") up to the first comma. For "Table 26 Defendants
finalised, Summary characteristics by court level – Queensland" the subject is
"Defendants finalised". Subjects are looked up in lower case, with footnote markers such
as "(a)" removed. A sheet without a title of its own reads each table's title (AIHW
sheets that stack "Table S126a", "S126b", …).

**`publication_subject_pattern`.** A publication's own pattern, used instead. The
Personal Safety surveys, prison health and young people returning to supervision keep
the second clause too, because their tables share the first: "Women aged 18 years and
over, Experiences of violence by a male perpetrator since the age of 15, By …" has the
subject "women aged 18 years and over, experiences of violence by a male perpetrator
since the age of 15". A "By …" clause is never part of it.

**`subjects`.** The subject map: publication → lower-case subject → fields to set.

```json
"criminal-courts-australia": {
  "defendants proven guilty (excluding traffic offences)": {
    "population": "Defendants with a guilty outcome", "scope": "Excluding traffic offences" } },
"prisoners-australia": {
  "crude imprisonment rate": { "population": "Prisoners", "measure": "Imprisonment rate", "rate_standardisation": "Crude" } }
```

The first version was generated by `orchestrator/qa/seeds/seed_measure_spec.R`, using
rules plus explicit overrides. These judgements are in it and can be changed here:
- `(excluding …)` becomes `scope`;
- court-level suffixes are dropped (harmonise takes `court_level` from the title);
- ABS's older "proven guilty" is treated as "with a guilty outcome";
- "FDV" is spelt out;
- combined "finalised and with a guilty outcome" tables count defendants finalised,
  because their `method_of_finalisation` or `population` labels separate the outcomes;
- summary tables of "all defendants" count defendants finalised, because the
  Criminal Courts collection counts finalised defendants.

**Victims publications.** Subjects for Crime Victimisation, Australia and Recorded Crime
– Victims were generated by `orchestrator/qa/seeds/seed_victims_specs.R`, using these
rules:
- **Crime Victimisation** (a survey): "persons aged 15 years and over who experienced X",
  "victims of X" and "household experience of X" become a population plus `crime_type =
  X`. Victims of personal crimes are persons aged 15 and over (18 and over for sexual
  assault); victims of household crimes are households. Other phrases become `scope`.
- **Recorded Crime – Victims:** "victims of X" becomes population "Victims" plus
  `victim_offence = X`, and an "Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander" prefix sets
  `indigenous_status`.

`'000` units give measure "Number".

**AIHW and RoGS.** Subjects for AIHW Child protection, AIHW Youth Justice and RoGS
Justice were generated by `orchestrator/qa/seeds/seed_aihw_rogs_specs.R`:
- **AIHW** titles name who is counted before "by …": "Children in out-of-home care by age
  group" gives population "Children in out-of-home care". Qualifiers stay in the
  population, so "on an average day" and "during the year" are separate populations.
  "Population of …" titles give measure "Population".
- **RoGS** titles name an indicator ("Backlog indicator", "Perceptions of safety"), so
  each has a hand-written population and measure. Survey indicators count persons aged 15
  and over; "(before 2023-24)" survey tables get scope "Survey before 2023–24".

**Group 2** (`orchestrator/qa/seeds/seed_national_specs.R`, ISSUES.md I37):
- **Personal Safety surveys and prison health:** the first clause is the population and
  the second the `scope` ("Experiences of violence by a male perpetrator since the age
  of 15", "Average number of births"). "Total women" and similar population labels are
  removed, so the title's population applies.
- **Personal Fraud:** "Experiences of card fraud in the last 12 months" counts persons
  aged 15 and over, with `crime_type` "Card fraud".
- **AIHW youth detention, returning, youth justice and child protection:** as AIHW above.
  A youth detention title's "First Nations" or "Non-Indigenous" prefix sets
  `indigenous_status`.
- **Corrective Services and RoGS 2026** are hand-written per title, as RoGS Justice.

**`replace_labels`.** Record labels that give way to the subject's value. `population:
"Total"` in a table's population column means the whole population the title names.
The normalisation spec maps "Total prisoners" and similar to "Total", so a Federal
Prisoners table's total becomes "Federal prisoners", not "Prisoners". A "Prisoners"
column heading gives way the same way ("UNSENTENCED PRISONERS, ..."), as do the family
and domestic violence populations, which subjects turn into `scope`.

`measure: "Proportion"` lets a title flag name a denominator. It applies wherever the
subject or a flag sets `measure`, so a subject must not set a measure that a labelled
`Proportion` in its tables should keep. Prisoners subjects no longer set `Number`, which
`default_measure` gives anyway.

**`title_flags`.** Regexes matched against the title and subtitle (joined by " / "). A
match sets the listed fields. Uses so far:
- ANZSOC 2023 tables (`offence_classification`);
- experimental data (`data_status`);
- aggregate sentence length vs expected time to serve (`sentence_basis`);
- "selected offences for federal prisoners" vs "for federal parolees" (`population`;
  the subject, "FEDERAL OFFENCES", doesn't say);
- what a Recorded Crime – Offenders police proceedings `Proportion` is a share of. It
  changed twice: of the offence (2008–09 to 2010–11), of all proceedings (the 2010–11
  combined table and 2011–12), of the method (2012–13 on, plain `Proportion`). The Total
  rows and columns show which;
- court level for the 2009–10 and 2010–11 Criminal Courts cubes, which name their
  court level only through the cube code in the subtitle (`45130DO002` …);
- AIHW title endings: Youth Justice "(rate)" (per 10,000 young people) and "(days)", and
  ": Number of receptions" and similar; Child protection "(number)" and "(per cent)";
  "age at first supervision" (`age_basis`); and child protection populations "as at 31
  December", a separate series from those at 30 June.

  `publication_title_flags` holds a publication's own flags, tried before these:
  Corrective Services' age-standardised cube is "At 30 June"; youth detention "on an
  average night" is `Average nightly number`; youth justice and child protection stacked
  tables are "(number)", "(%)", "(% of those with …)" or "(rate per 10,000)", and say
  what their year counts ("by year of most recent contact").

  The cube mapping (2 and 6 = Higher, 3 and 7 = Magistrates', 4 and 8 = Children's,
  5 = all courts) was checked against the data. 2008–09 cubes 2–4 follow the same
  mapping: matched against court-labelled series in other releases, their values agree
  with Higher Courts in 99%, Magistrates' in 96% and Children's in 96% of cases. 2008–09
  cubes 5–7 name their court level in their titles. In 2009–10, cubes 2 + 3 + 4 = cube 5
  for 707 of 816 cells. Cubes 6–8 of 2009–10 match the 2010–11 cubes that name their
  court level in 94–95% of overlapping cells.

**`measure_from_unit`.** A record still without `measure` takes one from its unit
(`"%"` → `Proportion`, `"no."` → `Number`, RoGS `"rate"` → `Rate`).

**`default_measure`.** Then the publication's default. It is set for the ABS justice
publications, AIHW Youth Justice and the group 2 AIHW publications and Corrective
Services, whose tables count people unless a label or title says otherwise. Child protection and RoGS have no default, so their gaps stay visible in
the report. A statistic in a
dimension (`sex: "Females %"`) needs a `split` entry, or it is stamped `Number`.

**`unit_from_measure`.** A record still without `unit` takes one from its measure
(`Number` → `no.`, `Proportion` → `%`). Measures whose unit differs by publication stay
out of it: `Victimisation rate` is a percentage in Crime Victimisation and a rate per
100,000 in Recorded Crime – Victims.

## Checking

`targets::tar_read(integration_gaps)$identity_conflicts` counts values that still can't
be told apart: the same series, period and release with different published text.
`numbers_differ` are real gaps in identification. `number_vs_symbol` are mostly
statistic rows that cross a rate panel (a mean age next to an `na` offender rate) and
are harmless.
